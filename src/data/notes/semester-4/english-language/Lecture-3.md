# Time Management

## Definition

**Time management** is the process of **planning, organizing, and controlling how you spend your time** so that you can achieve your goals efficiently.

It helps you decide:

- What activities need to be done
- Which activities are more important
- How much time to allocate to each activity
- When each activity should be completed

---

## Key Points

### Why Is Time Management Important?

Good time management helps you:

- Complete tasks on time
- Reduce stress
- Increase productivity
- Avoid procrastination
- Achieve academic and professional goals
- Maintain a healthy work-life balance
- Have more time for important activities

---

### First Approach to Time Management

The first approach is a simple **three-step process**:

#### 1. Plan the Activities

Write down the activities that need to be completed and allocate time for each one.

For example:

| Activity                       |    Time |
| ------------------------------ | ------: |
| Study JavaScript               | 2 hours |
| Complete university assignment |  1 hour |
| Practice coding                |  1 hour |

The activities can also be arranged as:

**First → Second → Third → ...**

#### 2. Prioritize the Activities

After planning, determine which activities should be completed first.

Activities can be categorized based on:

- **Urgent**
- **Important**
- **Not important**
- **Not urgent**

#### 3. Execute the Activities

Carry out the planned activities according to their priority and allocated time.

---

### Second Approach: Key Principles of Time Management

This approach provides more detailed principles:

1. **Set goals**
   - Know exactly what you want to achieve.

2. **Prioritize tasks**
   - Do the most important tasks first.

3. **Make a schedule**
   - Decide when you will perform each task.

4. **Avoid procrastination**
   - Do not unnecessarily delay important work.

5. **Eliminate distractions**
   - Reduce unnecessary phone use, social media, and interruptions.

6. **Break large tasks into smaller tasks**
   - A large assignment becomes easier when divided into manageable steps.

7. **Set deadlines**
   - Give yourself a specific time for completing each task.

8. **Review your progress**
   - At the end of the day or week, check what you accomplished and what remains.

---

### Activities vs. Tasks

An **activity** is usually broader, while a **task** is a specific action that makes up an activity.

**Example:**

- **Activity:** Cook Palau
- **Tasks:** Wash rice → Prepare ingredients → Cook rice → Add ingredients → Serve

So, **an activity can consist of multiple smaller tasks**.

## Explanation

The main idea of time management is not simply to **fill every hour with work**. It is to use available time intentionally.

A good process is:

**Set goals → Plan → Prioritize → Schedule → Execute → Review**

For example, instead of saying:

> "I need to study JavaScript."

You can turn it into a manageable plan:

**Goal:** Learn JavaScript OOP
↓
**Activity:** Study the OOP section
↓
**Tasks:** Watch lesson → Take notes → Code examples → Practice exercises
↓
**Schedule:** 5:30–8:00 AM
↓
**Review:** Check what was learned at the end of the day

This makes a large goal easier to manage.

## The Eisenhower Matrix

The **Eisenhower Matrix** is a framework for prioritizing tasks based on **urgency** and **importance**.

|                   | **Urgent**         | **Not Urgent**      |
| ----------------- | ------------------ | ------------------- |
| **Important**     | **Do First**       | **Schedule**        |
| **Not Important** | **Delegate/Limit** | **Eliminate/Limit** |

### 1. Important + Urgent

These tasks should be done **immediately**.

**Example:**

- Prepare for tomorrow's exam

### 2. Important + Not Urgent

These tasks should be **scheduled** because they are important even though they do not require immediate action.

**Example:**

- Work on long-term research
- Study for an exam several weeks in advance

### 3. Not Important + Urgent

These tasks may require attention but do not contribute significantly to your major goals.

**Example:**

- Answering every social-media message immediately

### 4. Not Important + Not Urgent

These tasks should generally be **limited or avoided** when they waste valuable time.

**Example:**

- Watching random videos without a specific purpose

## Common Mistakes

- **Planning without executing:** A schedule is useless if you do not follow it.
- **Overloading the schedule:** Do not assign more work than can realistically be completed.
- **Ignoring priorities:** Not every task has the same importance.
- **Procrastinating:** Delaying important work creates unnecessary pressure.
- **Too many distractions:** Frequent phone and social-media use reduces concentration.
- **Not breaking large tasks down:** Large tasks can feel overwhelming when treated as one unit.
- **No deadlines:** Without a clear deadline, tasks can easily be postponed.
- **Never reviewing progress:** You need to check whether your plan is actually working.
- **Confusing activities with tasks:** An activity is broad; tasks are the specific steps required to complete it.
- **Incorrect Eisenhower categorization:** Urgency and importance are different concepts.

## Short Exam Notes

- **Time management:** Planning, organizing, and controlling time to achieve goals efficiently.
- **Benefits:** On-time completion, less stress, higher productivity, less procrastination, goal achievement, better work-life balance.
- **First approach:** **Plan → Prioritize → Execute**
- **Second approach:** **Set goals → Prioritize → Schedule → Avoid procrastination → Eliminate distractions → Break tasks down → Set deadlines → Review**
- **Activity:** Broad piece of work.
- **Task:** Specific action within an activity.
- **Eisenhower Matrix:** Prioritizes work using **Urgent/Not Urgent** and **Important/Not Important**.
- **Important + Urgent:** Do first.
- **Important + Not Urgent:** Schedule.
- **Not Important + Urgent:** Limit/delegate when possible.
- **Not Important + Not Urgent:** Eliminate or minimize.

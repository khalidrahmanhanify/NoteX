# Email Writing

## Definition

Email writing is the process of composing and sending electronic messages to communicate with people for academic, professional, business, or personal purposes.

A well-written email should be clear, concise, polite, and appropriate for its audience and purpose.

## Key Points

### 1. Parts of an Email

A well-written email usually contains the following six parts, along with the recipient and subject fields.

1\. To: The recipient's email address.

- Example: `professor@example.com`

2\. Subject: A short phrase that explains the purpose of the email.

- Example: `Request for a Meeting`

3\. Greeting: A polite expression used to begin the email.

- Examples: `Dear Professor Ahmad,` or `Dear Sir or Madam,`

4\. Opening: Introduces the purpose of the email.

- Example: `I hope you are doing well. I am writing to request a meeting with you.`

5\. Body: Provides the necessary information, details, or request clearly and politely.

6\. Closing and Signature: Ends the email politely and identifies the sender.

- Example: `Thank you for your time and consideration.`
- `Best regards,`
- `Your Name`

### 2. Types of Email

A. Formal Email

Used for official or professional communication with employers, organizations, administrators, and other professional contacts.

Example: Applying for a teaching position.

B. Academic Email

Used to communicate with professors, teachers, supervisors, researchers, and universities about academic matters.

Example: Requesting research guidance from a professor.

C. Informal Email

Used to communicate with friends, relatives, and people with whom the sender has a close relationship.

Example: Sharing personal news with a friend.

Note: Academic emails are often formal in style, so formal and academic emails can overlap.

### 3. Useful Expressions for Email Writing

| Purpose                  | Useful expressions                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Greeting                 | Dear Professor Ahmad,                                  |
| Introducing the purpose  | I am writing to inquire about...                       |
| Making a request         | I would appreciate it if you could...                  |
| Asking for information   | Could you please provide further information about...? |
| Apologizing              | I sincerely apologize for...                           |
| Mentioning an attachment | Please find my CV attached.                            |
| Expressing thanks        | Thank you for your time and consideration.             |
| Closing                  | Best regards, / Kind regards, / Sincerely,             |

### 4. Rules for Effective Email Writing

- Use a clear and meaningful subject line.
- Address the recipient appropriately.
- State the purpose of the email early.
- Keep the message concise and organized.
- Use polite and professional language when appropriate.
- Check grammar, spelling, and punctuation.
- Attach the required files before sending.
- Include your name and relevant contact information when necessary.

## Example / Code

### Example 1: Requesting a Meeting

To: professor\@example.com

Subject: Request for a Meeting

Dear Professor Ahmad,

I hope this message finds you well.

I am writing to ask whether it would be possible to arrange a meeting with you to discuss my research proposal. I would appreciate the opportunity to receive your feedback and suggestions.

Please let me know a convenient date and time for you.

Thank you for your assistance.

Kind regards, [Your Name]

### Example 2: Asking for Admission Information

To: admissions\@example.com

Subject: Inquiry About Master's Program Admission

Dear Admissions Office,

I hope you are doing well.

I am interested in applying for the Master's program at your university. Could you please provide information about the admission requirements, application deadlines, tuition fees, and required documents?

I would also appreciate information about any available scholarships for international students.

Thank you for your time and assistance. I look forward to your response.

Sincerely, [Your Full Name]

### Example 3: Apologizing for a Late Assignment

Subject: Apology for Late Assignment Submission

Dear Professor,

I sincerely apologize for submitting my assignment after the deadline.

Due to unexpected circumstances, I was unable to complete and submit the work on time. I understand the importance of meeting deadlines, and I take responsibility for the delay.

I kindly ask you to consider accepting my assignment. I will make every effort to ensure that this does not happen again.

Thank you for your understanding.

Respectfully, [Your Name]

### Example 4: Requesting Leave

Subject: Request for Leave

Dear Sir/Madam,

I hope you are doing well.

I am writing to request leave for five days, from 10 October 2026 to 14 October 2026, due to personal reasons.

I will make every effort to complete any pending responsibilities before my absence. I would be grateful if you could approve my request.

Thank you for your understanding.

Kind regards, [Your Name]

### Example 5: Applying for a Job

Subject: Application for English Instructor Position

Dear Hiring Manager,

I hope this email finds you well.

I am writing to express my interest in the English Instructor position at your institution. I have a background in English Language Teaching and an M.Ed. in TESOL. I also have teaching experience in English language instruction, academic writing, and linguistics.

I believe my academic qualifications and teaching experience make me a suitable candidate for this position. I have attached my CV for your review.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to hearing from you.

Best regards, [Your Name] [Your Contact Number]

### Example 6: Requesting Academic Guidance

Subject: Request for Academic Guidance

Dear Professor Ahmad,

I hope you are doing well.

I am writing to request your guidance regarding my research interests and future academic plans. I would be grateful if you could provide me with some advice on selecting a suitable research topic and developing my research skills.

Your suggestions would be very valuable to me.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Best regards, [Your Name]

## Explanation

Email writing follows a logical process that helps the sender communicate effectively.

1. Identify the purpose: Decide whether you want to request something, ask for information, apologize, apply for a position, or communicate personal news.
2. Identify the audience: Choose the appropriate level of formality based on the recipient.
3. Write the subject: Summarize the purpose in a few words.
4. Begin with a greeting and opening: Address the recipient politely and explain why you are writing.
5. Develop the body: Provide the necessary details in a logical order.
6. Write the closing and signature: Thank the recipient when appropriate and include your name.
7. Review before sending: Check the recipient's address, subject, grammar, tone, attachments, and dates.

Following this process makes emails easier to understand and more professional.

## Common Mistakes

- Leaving the subject line empty or making it too vague.
- Using informal language in a formal email.
- Forgetting the greeting or signature.
- Writing long, disorganized paragraphs.
- Failing to explain the purpose of the email.
- Making grammar, spelling, or punctuation mistakes.
- Forgetting to attach a file mentioned in the message.
- Using an inappropriate greeting, such as addressing a professor as “Hey.”
- Using an unclear or incorrect date range in a leave request.
- Sending the email without reviewing it.

- Email writing: Writing electronic messages for communication.
- Main parts: To, Subject, Greeting, Opening, Body, Closing and Signature.
- Formal email: Used for official and professional purposes.
- Academic email: Used for educational and research purposes.
- Informal email: Used with friends and relatives.
- Subject: Briefly states the email's purpose.
- Greeting: Politely addresses the recipient.
- Opening: Introduces the reason for writing.
- Body: Contains the main message and details.
- Closing: Ends the email politely.
- Signature: Identifies the sender.
- Email writing process: Identify purpose → Identify audience → Write subject → Greeting and opening → Body → Closing and signature → Review.
- Important qualities: Clarity, conciseness, politeness, accuracy, and organization.

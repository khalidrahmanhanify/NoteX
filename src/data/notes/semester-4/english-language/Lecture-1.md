# English Tenses

## Definition

**Tense** shows the **time of an action or state**—whether it happens in the present, happened in the past, or will happen in the future.

English has **12 traditional tenses**, divided into four groups:

| Time        | Simple         | Continuous         | Perfect         | Perfect Continuous         |
| ----------- | -------------- | ------------------ | --------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Present** | Simple Present | Present Continuous | Present Perfect | Present Perfect Continuous |
| **Past**    | Simple Past    | Past Continuous    | Past Perfect    | Past Perfect Continuous    |
| **Future**  | Simple Future  | Future Continuous  | Future Perfect  | Future Perfect Continuous  |

This lecture covered the **first six tenses**:

1. Simple Present
2. Present Continuous
3. Simple Past
4. Past Continuous
5. Present Perfect
6. Present Perfect Continuous

---

## Key Points

### 1. Simple Present Tense

**Use:** Regular, repeated, habitual actions; facts and general truths.

**Common time expressions:**

- every day
- every week
- always
- usually
- often
- regularly
- frequently

**Affirmative:**

$$
Subject + V1 + Complement
$$

For **he, she, it**, the verb usually takes **-s, -es, or -ies**.

Examples:

- I go to university every day.
- They play tennis every week.
- She walks every morning.
- He washes his clothes.

**Negative:**

$$
Subject + do/does + not + V1 + Complement
$$

- I don't go to university every day.
- They don't play tennis.
- She doesn't walk every morning.
- He doesn't wash his clothes.

> After **doesn't**, always use the **base form (V1)**:

> ❌ He doesn't _washes_

> ✅ He doesn't _wash_

**Question:**

$$
Do/Does + Subject + V1 + Complement?
$$

- Do they play tennis?
- Does she walk every morning?
- Does he wash his clothes?

**Other uses:**

- **Schedules/timetables:** The class starts at 8 a.m. tomorrow.
- **News/headlines:** The president visits Russia next week.
- **Storytelling:** Historical events can sometimes be narrated using the present tense for vividness.

---

### 2. Present Continuous / Progressive Tense

**Use:** An action happening **now**, around the present time, temporary situations, planned near-future arrangements, and sometimes repeated actions that annoy the speaker.

**Affirmative:**

$$
Subject + am/is/are + V-ing + Complement
$$

- I am studying now.
- She is walking in the park.
- They are playing tennis.

**Negative:**

$$
Subject + am/is/are + not + V-ing + Complement
$$

- I am not studying.
- She is not walking.
- They are not playing tennis.

**Question:**

$$
Am/Is/Are + Subject + V-ing + Complement?
$$

- Am I studying?
- Is she walking?
- Are they playing tennis?

**Near-future arrangement:**

- Mohammad is traveling to India next month.
- I am going to Pakistan this summer.

**Temporary action:**

- We are writing an article these days.
- We are having many guests this week.

**Complaining/annoying repeated behavior:**

Usually used with words such as **always, constantly, forever, never**.

- You are always coming late.
- Your dog is constantly barking.

> This use often expresses **irritation**, rather than simply describing a normal repeated action.

---

### 3. Simple Past Tense

**Use:** Completed actions that happened in the past.

Common expressions:

- yesterday
- last night
- last year
- last summer
- two days ago
- in 2020

**Affirmative:**

$$
Subject + V2 + Complement
$$

- I visited Herat last year.
- She finished the lesson at 9.
- They arrived, ate, and left.
- He wrote his homework.
- We slept last night.

#### Regular and Irregular Verbs

**Regular verbs** form the past using patterns such as **-ed, -d, or -ied**.

| V1    | V2      |
| ----- | ------- |
| study | studied |
| book  | booked  |
| play  | played  |
| visit | visited |

**Irregular verbs** do not follow the regular **-ed** pattern.

| V1         | V2         | V3         |
| ---------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| understand | understood | understood |
| bite       | bit        | bitten     |
| fall       | fell       | fallen     |
| put        | put        | put        |
| write      | wrote      | written    |

**Negative:**

$$
Subject + did + not + V1 + Complement
$$

- I did not visit Herat.
- She did not finish the lesson.
- They did not arrive.
- He did not write his homework.

**Question:**

$$
Did + Subject + V1 + Complement?
$$

- Did you visit Herat?
- Did she finish the lesson?
- Did he write his homework?

> **Important:** After **did/didn't**, use **V1**, not V2.

> ❌ Did you _went_?

> ✅ Did you _go_?

---

### 4. Past Continuous / Progressive Tense

**Use:** An action that was **in progress at a particular time in the past**.

It is also commonly used when:

- one past action was interrupted by another action
- two actions were happening simultaneously

**Affirmative:**

$$
Subject + was/were + V-ing + Complement
$$

- I was reading at 8 p.m.
- She was cooking when I called.
- They were talking while I was working.
- We were playing football.

**Negative:**

$$
Subject + was/were + not + V-ing + Complement
$$

- I was not reading at 8 p.m.
- She was not cooking when I called.
- They were not talking while I was working.

**Question:**

$$
Was/Were + Subject + V-ing + Complement?
$$

- Was I reading at 8 p.m.?
- Was she cooking when I called?
- Were they talking?
- Were we playing football?

**Was/Were:**

- I → **was**
- He/She/It → **was**
- You/We/They → **were**

---

### 5. Present Perfect Tense

**Use:** Connects a **past action or situation with the present**.

The exact past time is usually **not specified**.

**Affirmative:**

$$
Subject + have/has + V3 + Complement
$$

- I have finished my work.
- She has visited Kabul twice.
- They have taken the test.
- We have started a new business.

**Have/Has:**

- I/You/We/They → **have**
- He/She/It → **has**

#### With "Since"

**Since** introduces the **starting point** of an action or situation that continues to the present.

- I have been sick **since last night**.
- She has been in Kabul **since March**.
- He has been happy **since 2020**.

#### With "For"

**For** introduces the **duration/length of time**.

- We have been married **for ten years**.
- I have worked here **for nine months**.
- We have watched cricket **for two hours**.

**Since = starting point**

> since 2020, since Monday, since morning

**For = duration**

> for two hours, for three years, for ten days

#### Present time period

Present Perfect can also be used when an action happened during a time period that **has not finished yet**.

- I have finished university **this week**.
- I have visited several places **this year**.

**Negative:**

$$
Subject + have/has + not + V3 + Complement
$$

- I have not finished my work.
- She has not visited Kabul.
- They have not taken the test.

**Question:**

$$
Have/Has + Subject + V3 + Complement?
$$

- Have I finished my work?
- Has he visited Kabul?
- Have they taken the test?

---

### 6. Present Perfect Continuous Tense

**Use:** An action that **started in the past and has continued up to now**, with emphasis on the **duration or ongoing activity**.

**Affirmative:**

$$
Subject + have/has + been + V-ing + Complement
$$

- I have been teaching for two hours.
- She has been studying since morning.
- They have been sleeping since 10 o'clock.

**Negative:**

$$
Subject + have/has + not + been + V-ing + Complement
$$

- I have not been teaching for two hours.
- She has not been studying since morning.
- They have not been sleeping since 10 o'clock.

**Question:**

$$
Have/Has + Subject + been + V-ing + Complement?
$$

- Have I been teaching for two hours?
- Has she been studying since morning?
- Have they been sleeping since 10 o'clock?

---

### Auxiliary and Main Verbs

**Auxiliary verbs** are helping verbs used with main verbs to form grammatical structures such as tenses, questions, and negatives.

Common auxiliary verbs include:

- **be** → am, is, are, was, were
- **do** → do, does, did
- **have** → have, has, had

**Main verbs** carry the main meaning of the sentence.

#### Dynamic Verbs

Dynamic verbs describe **actions or activities**.

- He plays cricket.
- He played cricket.
- She is studying.

#### Stative Verbs

Stative verbs describe a **state, condition, feeling, possession, thought, or existence** rather than an action.

- He is a doctor. → state
- I am happy. → state
- I am at home. → location/existence
- I know the answer. → mental state

> Many stative verbs are normally **not used in the continuous form**. For example:

> ✅ I know the answer.

> ❌ I am knowing the answer.

## Explanation

### Quick Comparison of the Six Tenses

| Tense                          | Main Idea                                | Example                             |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Simple Present**             | Habit/routine/fact                       | I study every day.                  |
| **Present Continuous**         | Happening now/temporary                  | I am studying now.                  |
| **Simple Past**                | Completed past action                    | I studied yesterday.                |
| **Past Continuous**            | Action in progress in the past           | I was studying at 8 p.m.            |
| **Present Perfect**            | Past action connected to present         | I have studied this topic.          |
| **Present Perfect Continuous** | Activity continuing from past to present | I have been studying for two hours. |

### The Most Important Differences

**Simple Present vs Present Continuous**

- I **study** English every day. → routine
- I **am studying** English now. → happening now

**Simple Past vs Past Continuous**

- I **studied** at 8 p.m. → completed action
- I **was studying** at 8 p.m. → action was in progress

**Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous**

- I **have studied** English for two years. → focuses more on the fact/result
- I **have been studying** English for two years. → emphasizes the ongoing activity/duration

### "Since" vs "For"

Think of it this way:

**Since → When did it start?**

> since Monday
> since 2020
> since morning

**For → How long?**

> for two hours
> for five years
> for three weeks

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using V2 after does/did**
   - ❌ She doesn't **walks**.
   - ✅ She doesn't **walk**.
   - ❌ Did you **went**?
   - ✅ Did you **go**?

2. **Forgetting -s/-es with third-person singular**
   - ❌ He play football.
   - ✅ He plays football.

3. **Using the wrong form of be**
   - ❌ They was studying.
   - ✅ They were studying.

4. **Forgetting "been" in Present Perfect Continuous**
   - ❌ I have studying for two hours.
   - ✅ I have been studying for two hours.

5. **Confusing since and for**
   - ❌ I have studied for 2020.
   - ✅ I have studied since 2020.
   - ❌ I have studied since two years.
   - ✅ I have studied for two years.

6. **Using Present Continuous for every present action**
   - ❌ I am knowing the answer.
   - ✅ I know the answer.

7. **Confusing V2 and V3**
   - Simple Past → **V2**: I went.
   - Present Perfect → **V3**: I have gone.

## Short Exam Notes

- **Simple Present:** `S + V1 (+ s/es) + C` → habits, routines, facts.
- **Present Continuous:** `S + am/is/are + V-ing` → happening now, temporary actions, arrangements.
- **Simple Past:** `S + V2 + C` → completed past actions.
- **Past Continuous:** `S + was/were + V-ing` → action in progress in the past.
- **Present Perfect:** `S + have/has + V3` → past action connected to present; unspecified time.
- **Present Perfect Continuous:** `S + have/has + been + V-ing` → action continuing from past until now.
- **Do/Does/Did + V1** in questions and negatives.
- **Have/Has + V3** → Present Perfect.
- **Have/Has + been + V-ing** → Present Perfect Continuous.
- **Since = starting point.**
- **For = duration.**
- **V2 = Simple Past.**
- **V3 = Past Participle**, used with **have/has/had** in perfect constructions.
- **Was** → I/he/she/it.
- **Were** → you/we/they.

2. **Sentence Structure and Grammar**

## Key Points

### 1. Simple Future Tense

The **Simple Future** is used to talk about:

- Actions that will happen in the future
- Predictions about the future

### Structure

**Affirmative:**

```text
Subject + will + V1 + complement
```

**Negative:**

```text
Subject + will not + V1 + complement
```

**Question:**

```text
Will + subject + V1 + complement?
```

### Examples

```text
I will call you tomorrow.
I will help you with the task.
I think it will rain.
Afghanistan will win the soccer match.
```

Negative:

```text
I will not call you tomorrow.
I will not help you with the task.
I think you will not pass the test.
```

Questions:

```text
Will I call tomorrow?
Will you pass the test?
Will Afghanistan win the match?
```

### `will` vs. `be going to`

Both can refer to the future, but they are commonly distinguished as:

- **will** → decisions/actions not previously planned or predictions
- **be going to** → planned or intended future actions

Examples:

```text
I am going to come.
We are going to have a class.
She is going to go home.
```

With `be going to`:

- **am** → I
- **is** → singular subjects
- **are** → plural subjects

---

### 2. Future Progressive / Continuous

The **Future Progressive** describes an action that will be **in progress at a particular time in the future**.

### Structure

**Affirmative:**

```text
Subject + will be + V-ing
```

**Negative:**

```text
Subject + will not be + V-ing
```

**Question:**

```text
Will + subject + be + V-ing?
```

### Examples

```text
I will be teaching at 1 o'clock.
She will be studying tonight.
They will be using the computer tomorrow.
```

Negative:

```text
I will not be teaching at 1 o'clock.
She will not be studying tonight.
They will not be using the computer tomorrow.
```

Question:

```text
Will I be teaching at 1 o'clock?
Will she be studying tonight?
Will they be using the computer tomorrow?
```

---

### 3. Past Perfect Tense

The **Past Perfect** describes an action that was **completed before another action in the past**.

### Structure

**Affirmative:**

```text
Subject + had + V3
```

**Negative:**

```text
Subject + had not + V3
```

**Question:**

```text
Had + subject + V3?
```

### Examples

```text
When I arrived, she had left.
They had finished before the teacher came.
He was tired because he had worked late.
```

Negative:

```text
When I arrived, she had not left.
They had not finished before the teacher came.
He was not tired because he had not worked late.
```

Questions:

```text
Had she left?
Had they finished their homework?
Had he gone home?
```

### Easy way to remember

```text
Earlier past action → Past Perfect
Later past action  → Simple Past
```

Example:

```text
She had left before I arrived.
    ↑                 ↑
 earlier           later
```

---

### 4. Past Perfect Progressive

The **Past Perfect Progressive** describes an activity that **had been continuing before another past action or time**.

### Structure

**Affirmative:**

```text
Subject + had been + V-ing
```

**Negative:**

```text
Subject + had not been + V-ing
```

**Question:**

```text
Had + subject + been + V-ing?
```

### Examples

```text
She had been studying before I turned off my laptop.
It had been raining before I finished the lecture.
He was tired because he had been working.
```

Negative:

```text
She had not been studying before I turned off my laptop.
It had not been raining before I finished the lecture.
He had not been working.
```

Question:

```text
Had she been studying before I turned off my laptop?
Had it been raining before I finished the lecture?
Had he been working?
```

---

### 5. Future Perfect Tense

The **Future Perfect** describes an action that **will be completed before a specific future time or another future event**.

### Structure

**Affirmative:**

```text
Subject + will have + V3
```

**Negative:**

```text
Subject + will not have + V3
```

**Question:**

```text
Will + subject + have + V3?
```

### Examples

```text
By Friday, I will have finished the course.
She will have arrived by 6 p.m.
By 2030, they will have built the new campus.
```

Negative:

```text
By Friday, I will not have finished the course.
She will not have arrived by 6 p.m.
By 2030, they will not have built the new campus.
```

Question:

```text
Will I have finished the course by Friday?
Will she have arrived by 6 p.m.?
Will they have built the new campus by 2030?
```

---

### 6. Future Perfect Continuous

The **Future Perfect Continuous** describes the **duration of an action up to a specific future time**.

### Structure

**Affirmative:**

```text
Subject + will have been + V-ing
```

**Negative:**

```text
Subject + will not have been + V-ing
```

**Question:**

```text
Will + subject + have been + V-ing?
```

### Examples

```text
By June, I will have been teaching for ten years.
At 5 p.m., she will have been studying for three hours.
Next month, they will have been working here for a year.
```

Negative:

```text
By June, I will not have been teaching for ten years.
At 5 p.m., she will not have been studying for three hours.
Next month, they will not have been working here for a year.
```

Questions:

```text
Will I have been teaching English for ten years?
Will he have been going home every day?
Will they have been playing cricket every night?
```

---

### Tense Comparison

| Tense                     | Main Idea                            | Structure                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Simple Future             | Future action/prediction             | `will + V1`              |
| Future Continuous         | Action in progress in future         | `will be + V-ing`        |
| Past Perfect              | Completed before another past action | `had + V3`               |
| Past Perfect Continuous   | Ongoing before another past action   | `had been + V-ing`       |
| Future Perfect            | Completed before future time         | `will have + V3`         |
| Future Perfect Continuous | Duration up to future time           | `will have been + V-ing` |

### 7. Sentence

A **sentence** is a group of words that communicates a **complete concept or thought**.

A complete sentence normally contains:

```text
Subject + Predicate
```

It:

- Begins with a capital letter.
- Communicates a complete idea.
- Ends with appropriate punctuation.

Example:

```text
The students study English.
```

Here:

```text
Subject   → The students
Predicate → study English
```

---

### 8. Essential Parts of a Sentence

There are two essential parts:

#### Subject

The subject tells us **who or what** the sentence is about.

Example:

```text
The students study English.
      ↑
   Subject
```

#### Predicate

The predicate tells us **what the subject does, is, or experiences**.

```text
The students | study English.
   Subject   |   Predicate
```

Examples:

```text
I watch TV every night.
They wash their clothes.
He will come next week.
We should write an article.
The students completed the assignment.
```

---

### 9. Clause

A **clause** is a group of words containing a **subject and a verb**.

```text
Clause = Subject + Verb
```

There are two main types:

#### Independent Clause

An independent/main clause:

- Has a subject and verb.
- Expresses a complete meaning.
- Can stand alone as a sentence.

Example:

```text
He goes home.
```

#### Dependent Clause

A dependent/subordinate clause:

- Has a subject and verb.
- Does **not** express a complete meaning by itself.
- Usually depends on an independent clause.

Examples:

```text
Because she works
If he studies hard
After I finished my work
Before he called me
As we woke up
```

For example:

```text
Because she works, she earns money.
```

`Because she works` is dependent, while `she earns money` is independent.

---

### 10. Subordinating Conjunctions

These words commonly introduce dependent clauses.

| Purpose   | Conjunctions                                  |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Time      | when, while, before, after, until, as soon as |
| Reason    | because, since, as                            |
| Condition | if, unless, provided that                     |
| Contrast  | although, though, even though, whereas        |
| Purpose   | so that, in order that                        |

Example:

```text
Although it was raining, we went to class.
```

---

### 11. Simple Sentence

A **simple sentence** contains **one independent clause**.

It can have:

- A simple or compound subject
- A simple or compound verb

But it has only **one independent clause**.

Examples:

```text
The teacher explained the lesson.
Ali studied chemistry.
We practiced simple present tense.
They play football.
Jawed watched a movie.
```

---

### 12. Compound Sentence

A **compound sentence** contains **two or more independent clauses**.

The clauses can be joined using:

- Coordinating conjunctions
- A semicolon

### FANBOYS

```text
F = for
A = and
N = nor
B = but
O = or
Y = yet
S = so
```

Examples:

```text
They watched a movie, and we slept.

I helped him, but he betrayed me.

You can live here, or you can go.

She studied hard, yet she failed the test.
```

A semicolon can also join closely related independent clauses:

```text
The weather was cold; we stayed indoors.
```

---

### 13. Complex Sentence

A **complex sentence** contains:

```text
1 Independent Clause + at least 1 Dependent Clause
```

Examples:

```text
I went home because I was tired.

If I become the supreme leader, I will open girls' schools.

Although she was tired, she continued studying.
```

### Comma Rule

When the dependent clause comes **first**, use a comma:

```text
Because I was tired, I went home.
```

When the independent clause comes first, a comma is generally not needed before a dependent clause beginning with a subordinating conjunction:

```text
I went home because I was tired.
```

---

### Sentence Type Comparison

| Type     | Structure                            |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Simple   | 1 independent clause                 |
| Compound | 2+ independent clauses               |
| Complex  | 1 independent + 1+ dependent clauses |

---

### 14. Punctuation

Punctuation marks help make the meaning of written language clear.

#### Period `.`

Used at the end of a statement.

```text
The teacher explained the lesson.
```

It can also end an imperative:

```text
Sit down.
Please stand up.
```

---

#### Question Mark `?`

Used at the end of a **direct question**.

```text
What is your name?
Where do you live?
Can you speak English?
```

A statement can sometimes be spoken with rising intonation to function as a question:

```text
He is your brother?
```

---

#### Exclamation Mark `!`

Used to express **strong feeling or emphasis**.

```text
I got the first position!
We must go to class on time!
```

In formal/academic writing, excessive exclamation marks should generally be avoided.

---

#### Comma `,`

Common uses include:

1. Separating items in a list
2. Following an introductory dependent clause
3. Following an introductory phrase
4. Before a coordinating conjunction joining independent clauses

Examples:

```text
He bought a pen, a book, a bag, a chair, and a desk.

Because he didn't study, he failed the test.

Unfortunately, I failed the test.

He went to school, but I played soccer.
```

---

#### Semicolon `;`

A semicolon can join two **closely related independent clauses**.

```text
We went home; they stayed at the university.

She studied hard; she passed the interview.

Ahmad opened the door; I entered the room.
```

---

#### Colon `:`

A colon can introduce a list or explanation.

```text
He bought the following items: a pen, a book, a bag, and a desk.
```

---

### 15. Avoiding Run-ons and Comma Splices

A **comma splice** occurs when two independent clauses are joined only with a comma.

Incorrect:

```text
I was tired, I went home.
```

This can be corrected in several ways.

#### Period

```text
I was tired. I went home.
```

#### Semicolon

```text
I was tired; I went home.
```

#### FANBOYS

```text
I was tired, so I went home.
```

---

### 16. Capitalization

Important capitalization rules include:

#### First Word of a Sentence

```text
He is a professor.
```

Not:

```text
he is a professor.
```

#### Pronoun `I`

Always capitalize `I`.

```text
I am an engineer.
Who am I?
```

#### Proper Nouns

Names of specific people, places, institutions, etc.

```text
Afghanistan
Kabul
Kardan University
```

#### Days, Months, and Holidays

```text
Monday
August
Eid
24th Asad
```

#### Names and Titles

```text
Professor Ahmad
Jawed
Fatime
Putin
Zabihullah
```

---

### 17. Capitalization in Titles

Important words in titles are generally capitalized.

Examples:

```text
World War Two
Presidential Election
World Cup
Life in a Jungle
Life and Death
A King with No Power
On Mars
Now and Then
```

Generally, short:

- Articles: `a`, `an`, `the`
- Prepositions: `in`, `on`, `from`, `out`, etc.
- FANBOYS: `for`, `and`, `nor`, `but`, `or`, `yet`, `so`

are not capitalized when they occur in the middle of a title, unless the applicable style guide says otherwise.

## Explanation

### The Six Tenses at a Glance

A useful way to understand them is to focus on **time + action**:

```text
SIMPLE FUTURE
Future action
        ↓
I will study.

FUTURE CONTINUOUS
Action in progress in the future
        ↓
I will be studying.

PAST PERFECT
Completed before another past action
        ↓
I had studied before the exam.

PAST PERFECT CONTINUOUS
Ongoing before another past action
        ↓
I had been studying before the exam.

FUTURE PERFECT
Completed before a future time
        ↓
I will have studied by Friday.

FUTURE PERFECT CONTINUOUS
Duration continuing up to a future time
        ↓
I will have been studying for three hours by 8 p.m.
```

### Sentence Structure

Remember the hierarchy:

```text
Sentence
   ↓
contains
   ↓
Clause(s)
   ↓
each clause contains
   ↓
Subject + Verb
```

Then classify the sentence based on its clauses:

```text
1 Independent clause
       ↓
    Simple

2+ Independent clauses
       ↓
    Compound

1 Independent + 1+ Dependent
       ↓
    Complex
```

## Common Mistakes

### Tenses

- Using `V1` after `had` instead of `V3`.
  - ❌ `had went`
  - ✅ `had gone`

- Forgetting `-ing` in continuous tenses.
  - ❌ `will be study`
  - ✅ `will be studying`

- Forgetting `been` in perfect continuous tenses.
  - ❌ `will have teaching`
  - ✅ `will have been teaching`

- Using `will + V-ing`.
  - ❌ `will studying`
  - ✅ `will be studying`

### Sentences

- A dependent clause cannot normally stand alone.
  - ❌ `Because I was late.`
  - ✅ `Because I was late, I missed the class.`

- Do not join two independent clauses with only a comma.
  - ❌ `I was tired, I went home.`
  - ✅ `I was tired, so I went home.`

- Do not confuse a **compound** sentence with a **complex** sentence:
  - Compound → independent + independent
  - Complex → independent + dependent

### Punctuation & Capitalization

- ❌ `i live in kabul.`

- ✅ `I live in Kabul.`

- ❌ `we have class on monday.`

- ✅ `We have class on Monday.`

- Avoid unnecessary exclamation marks in academic writing.

- Remember that a semicolon requires independent clauses on both sides.

- When an introductory dependent clause comes first, normally place a comma after it.

# Communication Skills

## Definition

Communication is the process of sharing information, ideas, thoughts, and feelings between people to create mutual understanding.

The word communication comes from the Latin word communicare, which means “to share” or “to make common.”

Communication skills are the abilities we use to send, receive, understand, and respond to messages effectively.

Effective communication means expressing a message clearly and understanding the message of another person.

## Key Points

### 1. Importance of Communication Skills

Communication skills are important because they:

1. Build positive relationships: Help people develop trust and maintain good relationships.
2. Improve teaching and learning: Help teachers explain concepts and students understand lessons.
3. Reduce misunderstandings and conflicts: Make messages clearer and prevent confusion.
4. Support teamwork and leadership: Help people cooperate and achieve common goals.
5. Increase confidence: Help people express their thoughts and opinions confidently.

### 2. The Communication Process

The communication process consists of four main elements:

1. Sender: The person who creates and sends the message.
2. Message: The information, idea, or feeling being communicated.
3. Receiver: The person who receives and interprets the message.
4. Feedback: The receiver's response to the message.

### 3. Types of Communication

The main types covered in this session are:

- Verbal communication
- Non-verbal communication
- Written communication

Listening is also an essential communication skill.

#### A. Verbal Communication

Verbal communication involves using spoken language to share information, ideas, and feelings.

Key points:

- Use clear and appropriate vocabulary.
- Speak at an understandable speed and volume.
- Organize ideas logically.
- Use language appropriate to the situation and audience.

Example: A student explains a lesson to classmates.

#### B. Listening (Auditory Communication)

Listening is the process of paying attention to spoken messages, understanding them, and responding appropriately.

Key points:

- Listen carefully without interrupting.
- Identify the speaker's main ideas and important details.
- Ask questions when something is unclear.
- Give appropriate feedback.
- Maintain suitable eye contact when culturally appropriate.

Example: A student listens to a teacher's explanation and asks a question to clarify a difficult concept.

#### C. Non-Verbal Communication

Non-verbal communication involves conveying information without relying on words alone.

Examples include:

- Facial expressions: Smiling may express happiness or friendliness.
- Eye contact: Can show attention and engagement.
- Body language: Posture and gestures can communicate confidence or discomfort.
- Tone of voice: Can express emotions such as excitement, anger, or sadness.
- Stress and pauses: Emphasizing certain words or pausing can change the meaning or impact of a message.

#### D. Written Communication

Written communication involves sharing information through written words.

Examples: Emails, reports, assignments, text messages, and academic writing.

Key points:

- Be clear, concise, and accurate.
- Organize information logically.
- Use language suitable for the audience.
- Check grammar, spelling, and punctuation.
- Use appropriate formatting.

### 4. Interpersonal Communication and Feedback

Interpersonal communication is the exchange of information between two or more people.

Effective interpersonal communication requires:

- Respect: Accept that people may have different opinions.
- Empathy: Try to understand another person's feelings and point of view.
- Constructive feedback: Give specific suggestions that help someone improve.
- Accepting feedback: Listen to suggestions without becoming unnecessarily defensive.

Example of constructive feedback:

“Your main idea is clear; adding an example would make it stronger.”

### 5. Barriers to Effective Communication

Communication barriers are factors that prevent a message from being sent, received, or understood correctly.

| Barrier                | Explanation                                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Language difficulties  | People may not understand the same language or vocabulary.                   |
| Poor listening         | The receiver does not pay sufficient attention.                              |
| Unclear messages       | The sender does not express ideas clearly or completely.                     |
| Cultural differences   | Different cultural norms may cause misunderstandings.                        |
| Noise and distractions | Environmental sounds or interruptions make communication difficult.          |
| Emotional reactions    | Anger, fear, or stress may affect understanding.                             |
| Misunderstandings      | The receiver interprets the message differently from the sender's intention. |

## Example / Code

### Example 1: The Communication Process

A teacher explains an assignment to a student.

- Sender: Teacher
- Message: Instructions for completing the assignment
- Receiver: Student
- Feedback: The student asks a question or explains the instructions back to the teacher.

### Example 2: Effective Communication

A student gives a presentation using clear language, maintains appropriate eye contact, organizes ideas logically, and answers questions politely.

This is effective communication because the student expresses ideas clearly and responds appropriately to the audience.

## Explanation

Effective communication requires more than simply speaking. The sender must express the message clearly, and the receiver must listen, interpret, and respond appropriately.

For example, a student might understand a topic but fail to communicate it clearly because the explanation is disorganized. Similarly, a teacher might explain a lesson clearly, but a distracted student may misunderstand it.

Therefore, effective communication depends on clear expression, active listening, appropriate non-verbal behavior, and useful feedback.

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing verbal communication with non-verbal communication.
- Assuming that communication only involves speaking.
- Thinking that hearing and listening mean the same thing. Hearing is the physical ability to perceive sounds; listening involves paying attention and understanding.
- Ignoring feedback during communication.
- Using unclear language or inappropriate vocabulary.
- Interrupting others instead of listening carefully.
- Giving criticism without offering constructive suggestions.
- Ignoring cultural differences and emotional barriers.
